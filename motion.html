Motion

Everything moves on one tempo

Iqaa runs at 100 BPM: one beat is 600 ms. Its quarter and half beats, 150 and 300 ms, are the durations interfaces already use; brand moments take whole beats.

Tokens

Durations from the tempo

TokenDurationUse
--m-eighth75 msStagger between items in a sequence
--m-quarter150 msSmall changes: a stroke is written, a check appears, a hover
--m-half300 msA job moves to the next station; a panel opens
--m-beat600 msBrand moments: the mark plays, a handoff completes
--m-bar2400 msThe longest anything may take: four beats

Principles

How things move

  1. Step, hold, release. Things move on the beat and then stop. Nothing drifts, floats or loops on its own.
  2. Nothing moves without a cause. A person pressed something, or a job moved. Decoration never animates.
  3. Arrive slowing down, leave speeding up. No overshoot, no elastic, no bounce. Composed, not cute.
  4. Only what changed moves. The rest of the screen holds still, so the eye finds the change.
  5. Reduced motion replaces every move with an instant change and keeps every message.

Sound

A rule for later

Written, not produced, so the product can add it later on the same rule. A notification sound plays the event as beats at 100 BPM: a short low tap for a step that ran, a single long tone for something waiting for you, a plucked tone for a person’s action. Never longer than one bar; never a melody; off by default.
