Three new ways to draw the line

The idea stays: a line is written as steps, a person’s step differs from a machine’s by shape and by violet, and every organization’s line becomes its signature. Only the drawing changes. Below each mark: the same catering line as before, written in that direction.

A · Recommended

The signature التوقيع

One continuous pen line. A step that runs by itself is a straight stroke; a person’s step is a loop of the hand; a wait for a person is an open arch. Every line literally signs its own name.

Sufra’s catering line, written in it

  • Most ownable: no automation brand draws a hand’s stroke.
  • Survives 16 px as one line with one loop.
  • Mirrors cleanly for Arabic: the pen simply runs right to left.
  • Risk: a loop can read as a cursive l; kept to one loop in the mark.

Nuqta and kashida النقطة والتطويل

From Arabic calligraphy: the line is a stretched baseline, and the steps are the calligrapher’s rhombus dots — above the line for a person, below it for a machine. The mark carries the dots of إيقاع itself.

Sufra’s catering line, written in it

  • Deepest Arabic root; a quiet, typographic look.
  • Dots read clearly from 20 px up.
  • Risk: two dots and a dash can read as ÷ at a glance.
  • Latin readers see pattern, not meaning.

The weave النول

The job is a violet thread passing through the stations: over a warp when a person works it, under when a machine does. The mark is one stitch through three hands.
