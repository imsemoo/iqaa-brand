Signature

Every line signs its own name

The pen that draws Iqaa’s mark writes any line. A kitchen, a software house and a video studio get three different signatures from the same strokes — and each one is the true shape of its work.

Grammar

Six states, told apart by the stroke

RunsA step that runs by itself: a message, a reminder, an invoice. A straight stroke, ink.
WaitsThe line waits for a person: an approval, a review, a Watar draft. An open arch, violet.
By handA person does the work. A loop of the hand, violet.
PauseA timed wait. The pen lifts and leaves a dot, ink.

Failed and Test belong to the product, never to the mark. A failed step snaps, in the alert colour. In a test, or in a draft Watar has not had approved, nothing is signed yet: the whole line is drawn with a dashed grey pen and nothing turns violet.

Rules

How a line becomes a signature

  1. One step is one stretch of the pen. Its kind sets its shape: a step that runs by itself is a straight stroke; a task for a person is a loop of the hand; an approval, a review or a Watar draft — a step that waits for a person — is an open arch; a timed wait lifts the pen and leaves a dot.
  2. Steps follow the order of the line, on one baseline. Between stations the pen runs half a step of straight: the handoff.
  3. The pen never shrinks to fit: a long line is drawn smaller, never cramped.
  4. An organization’s mark is its initial on an ink tile, signed underneath with the opening of its own line: whole stations, up to three steps.
  5. Publishing a new version of a line changes its signature. Jobs already running keep the version — and the signature — they started on.
  6. Arabic: the same strokes, written right to left; the loops turn the other way.
  7. On ink, strokes that run by themselves turn white and a person’s strokes lilac. Nothing else changes.

    Use

    Where signatures go

    Where a signature appears

    • The organization’s mark, when it has no logo of its own.
    • The sign-in screen of its subdomain, and the header of the link its clients open.
    • The cover of its reports and exports.
    • The template picker, so a kitchen and a studio can be told apart at a glance.
    • Iqaa’s own marketing, only when it shows a real or demo line.

    Where it never appears

    • As a repeating pattern or wallpaper.
    • Behind text, at any opacity.
    • Writing itself on a loop. The pen moves only when a line is signed or a job moves.
    • On anything that is not about a line.
    • Invented: a signature always comes from an actual line, or a labelled demo.
